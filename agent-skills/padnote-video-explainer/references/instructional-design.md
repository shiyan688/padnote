# Instructional design

- Give every scene one observable learning objective.
- Use narration to explain the reasoning; use screen text as labels, anchors, or summaries. Do not repeat a narrated sentence verbatim on screen.
- Introduce notation before transforming it. For `formula_steps`, reveal one justified step at a time.
- Use `process` for ordered actions, `concept_map` for relationships or cycles, and `comparison` only when the contrast itself teaches the distinction.
- Preserve incomplete or doubtful source statements as uncertain claims. Do not silently repair them with outside knowledge.
- Keep the number of scenes and visual items small enough to read inside the 9:16 safe area. The storyboard layout gate is authoritative.
- Explain the teaching purpose in every `animation_intent.explanation`; decorative motion is not sufficient.

## Explanatory quality

Use the audience's likely misconception to choose the teaching sequence. Start from a concrete question or observation, make the relevant relationship visible, then introduce notation and test transfer with a small changed example. Adapt this sequence when the material warrants it; do not force every lesson into an identical number of scenes.

For quantitative topics, preserve units, scale and reference quantities. For example, equal percentage changes need visibly different bases; a derivative needs a changing secant interval; frequency decomposition needs components and their sum. Text cards that merely name these relationships are not equivalent to showing them.

Judge animation by what a viewer can infer from the change. Fades and sequential bullet reveals can guide attention, but do not demonstrate proportional change, convergence, or superposition. If the supported renderer cannot express the essential relationship, report that limitation in the review rather than claiming high explanatory quality or inserting unsupported visual types.

Keep advanced lessons honest about prerequisites and scope. Separate an intuitive example from the general theorem and its conditions. Use mature educational works as a benchmark for explanatory clarity, not as layouts, scripts, or an assertion that generated output meets their quality.
